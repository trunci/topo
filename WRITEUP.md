# Topology of Attention — Consolidated Writeup

**One-line result:** a transformer's attention-graph topology carries a *real,
reproducible* signal about reasoning (Spike, GREEN), but that signal is **not
prescriptive** for pruning (Experiment 1, NULL) and **not mechanistically specific** —
it does not localize a known circuit better than a trivial distance heuristic
(Experiment 2, RED). Net: topology correlates with reasoning structure, but we have no
evidence it captures anything a cheap first-order statistic does not.

All numbers in this document are generated by `src/write_writeup.py` from
`results/real_stats.json` (spike), `results/exp1_stats.json` (Exp 1), and
`results/exp2_stats.json` (Exp 2). No value is hand-typed.

---

## 1. Setup (shared)

- **Models:** Qwen2.5-0.5B(-Instruct), 24 layers x 14 heads (Spike, Exp 1);
  GPT-2 small, 12 x 12 = 144 heads (Exp 2). Eager attention, float32, CPU/MPS.
- **Topology pipeline:** per (layer, head) attention matrix -> max-symmetrize -> top-k
  sparsify -> flag (clique) complex -> persistent homology (H1) or discrete Morse matching.
- **Probes:** controlled 1-hop/2-hop minimal pairs (Spike); WikiText-2 perplexity +
  budget-matched pruning baselines (Exp 1); repeated-random induction detector (Exp 2).

## 2. Result A — Topology tracks reasoning (Spike): GREEN

40320 head-observations.

## 4. Result C — Topology does NOT match induction circuits (Exp 2): RED

Do topologically distinctive heads coincide with induction heads (detected independently)?
GPT-2 small, 144 heads.

- Discrimination: Spearman(induction, H1 persistence) rho = 0.114, p = 0.17191.
- Separation: top-10 induction heads vs rest, Mann-Whitney p = 0.01960
  (mean H1 0.316 vs 0.232).
- Beats trivial baseline? Spearman(induction, attention distance) rho = 0.446,
  p = 0.00000. Topology not dominated by distance: False.

The cheap attention-distance scalar predicts induction *better* than H1 persistence does.
**Verdict:** RED: no significant topology-induction correspondence.

## 5. Synthesis

The three results are coherent. Topology **locates** where reasoning structure lives
(A), but the discrete-Morse critical set is **not** the quality-preserving subgraph (B),
and topological distinctiveness is **not** a better circuit-locator than a one-line
attention statistic (C). The defensible contribution is narrow: H1 persistence is a real
but **first-order-redundant** correlate of reasoning structure. The strong proposal
claims (topological pruning; topology predicts circuits) are not supported at this scale.

## 6. Honest caveats

- Exp 1 and Exp 2 were small, memory-safe CPU runs; directions are clear but magnitudes
  are not nailed down. The Spike is the largest and most robust run.
- Mean PPL = mean(exp(loss)) is outlier-dominated; the paired per-example loss test agrees
  with the NULL verdict regardless.
- "Tracks reasoning" (Spike) was measured on *attempted* reasoning (attention over the
  prompt), not gated on correct answers.
- Single model per experiment; one circuit (induction) tested in Exp 2.

## 7. Follow-up research directions

Four candidates, roughly ordered by how directly they build on what we have:

1. **Residual-information test (is topology redundant?).** The sharpest open question from
   Exp 2: does H1 persistence add *anything* beyond first-order attention statistics (mean
   distance, entropy, off-diagonal mass)? Regress the reasoning/circuit signal on those
   scalars and test whether topology has residual predictive power. If it never does, the
   honest paper is "topological features of attention are redundant with simple statistics."
   Cheap; reuses all existing data. **Highest information-per-compute.**

2. **Failure prediction (Spike's payoff / proposal Exp 3).** Train a probe on per-example
   topological features to predict reasoning errors on held-out items. This is the
   application the Spike's positive result actually supports, and it is the only proposal
   claim still untested. Moderate cost; strongest evidence base.

3. **Beyond-H1 / better descriptors.** We used only the crudest summary (scalar total
   persistence). Try H0 (attention clusters), persistence images/landscapes as feature
   vectors, and honest graph-statistic competitors (spectral gap, modularity, motifs). If a
   $10 graph statistic matches topology, that reframes the project.

4. **Sheaves over the residual stream (proposal fallback #2).** The flag complex discards
   *what* flows along edges. A cellular sheaf with stalks from OV-circuit subspaces keeps
   the linear-algebraic content; sheaf cohomology may capture structure pure graph topology
   cannot. Highest-risk, highest-ceiling; most new machinery.
