# Topology of Attention — Consolidated Writeup

**One-line result:** a transformer's attention-graph topology carries a *real,
reproducible* signal about reasoning (Spike, GREEN). It is **not prescriptive** for pruning
(Exp 1, NULL) and its *marginal* correlation with a known circuit is weak (Exp 2, RED) — but
once first-order attention statistics are properly controlled for, topology carries
**significant residual signal** about that circuit (Exp 3, GREEN). Net: topology is a real,
**non-redundant** correlate of reasoning/circuit structure, but not a usable pruning
criterion at this scale.

All numbers in this document are generated by `src/write_writeup.py` from
`results/real_stats.json` (spike), `results/exp1_stats.json` (Exp 1),
`results/exp2_stats.json` (Exp 2), and `results/exp3_stats.json` (Exp 3). No value is
hand-typed.

---

## 1. Setup (shared)

- **Models:** Qwen2.5-0.5B(-Instruct), 24 layers x 14 heads (Spike, Exp 1);
  GPT-2 small, 12 x 12 = 144 heads (Exp 2). Eager attention, float32, CPU/MPS.
- **Topology pipeline:** per (layer, head) attention matrix -> max-symmetrize -> top-k
  sparsify -> flag (clique) complex -> persistent homology (H1) or discrete Morse matching.
- **Probes:** controlled 1-hop/2-hop minimal pairs (Spike); WikiText-2 perplexity +
  budget-matched pruning baselines (Exp 1); repeated-random induction detector (Exp 2).

## 4. Result C — Topology does NOT match induction circuits (Exp 2): RED

Do topologically distinctive heads coincide with induction heads (detected independently)?
GPT-2 small, 144 heads.

- Discrimination: Spearman(induction, H1 persistence) rho = 0.114, p = 0.17191.
- Separation: top-10 induction heads vs rest, Mann-Whitney p = 0.01960
  (mean H1 0.316 vs 0.232).
- Beats trivial baseline? Spearman(induction, attention distance) rho = 0.446,
  p = 0.00000. Topology not dominated by distance: False.

On its own, the cheap attention-distance scalar has a higher *marginal* correlation with
induction than H1 persistence does. **Verdict:** RED: no significant topology-induction correspondence.

But "marginal" is the key caveat — see Result D, which controls for the first-order scalars
jointly and reaches the opposite conclusion.

---

## 5. Result D — Topology is NOT redundant with first-order statistics (Exp 3): GREEN

Result C compared H1 against attention distance *one variable at a time*. The sharper test:
does H1 persistence add predictive power for induction **beyond** the first-order scalars
(attn_distance, offdiag_mass, attn_entropy) taken together? Nested OLS on the 144 GPT-2 heads.

- Baseline OLS (first-order scalars only): R2 = 0.137.
- Full OLS (+ H1 persistence): R2 = 0.211 → delta-R2 = 0.074.
- Nested-model F-test for the H1 term: F = 12.98, p = 0.00044.
- Partial Spearman(H1, induction | controls): rho = 0.372, p = 0.0000044
  (vs raw marginal rho = 0.114).

This is a **suppression effect**: H1's weak marginal correlation (Result C) was masked by its
own correlation with the first-order scalars; controlling for them *reveals* a strong partial
association. **Verdict:** GREEN: H1 persistence adds significant predictive power for induction beyond first-order attention statistics.

## 6. Synthesis

Topology **locates** where reasoning structure lives (A) and carries **genuine,
non-redundant** information about a known circuit once confounds are controlled (D) — its
weak marginal correlation in (C) was a suppression artifact, not redundancy. What topology
does *not* do is serve as a useful pruning criterion: the discrete-Morse critical set is not
the quality-preserving subgraph (B). So the defensible contribution is a **diagnostic** one —
H1 persistence is a real, non-trivial correlate of reasoning/circuit structure — while the
proposal's *prescriptive* claim (topological pruning) is unsupported at this scale. Note (C)
and (D) are not contradictory: (C) is a marginal comparison, (D) a partial one; (D) is the
more appropriate test and supersedes the "redundant" reading of (C).

## 7. Honest caveats

- Exp 1, Exp 2, and Exp 3 were small, memory-safe CPU runs (Exp 2/3 share one GPT-2 batch of
  144 heads); directions are clear but magnitudes are not nailed down. The Spike is the
  largest and most robust run.
- Mean PPL = mean(exp(loss)) is outlier-dominated; the paired per-example loss test agrees
  with the NULL verdict regardless.
- "Tracks reasoning" (Spike) was measured on *attempted* reasoning (attention over the
  prompt), not gated on correct answers.
- Exp 3's residual signal is shown for one circuit (induction) in one model; whether the
  partial association generalizes across circuits/models is untested.
- Single model per experiment.

## 8. Follow-up research directions

Four candidates, roughly ordered by how directly they build on what we have:

1. **Residual-information test (is topology redundant?).** ✅ DONE — this became Exp 3
   (Result D above): H1 persistence is *not* redundant with first-order statistics for
   induction (delta-R2 = 0.074, F-test p = 0.00044, partial rho = 0.372).
   Natural extension: repeat across more circuits and a larger head sample to test generality.

2. **Failure prediction (Spike's payoff / proposal Exp 3').** Train a probe on per-example
   topological features to predict reasoning errors on held-out items. This is the
   application the Spike's positive result actually supports, and it is the only proposal
   claim still untested. Moderate cost; strongest evidence base.

3. **Beyond-H1 / better descriptors.** We used only the crudest summary (scalar total
   persistence). Try H0 (attention clusters), persistence images/landscapes as feature
   vectors, and honest graph-statistic competitors (spectral gap, modularity, motifs). If a
   $10 graph statistic matches topology, that reframes the project.

4. **Sheaves over the residual stream (proposal fallback #2).** The flag complex discards
   *what* flows along edges. A cellular sheaf with stalks from OV-circuit subspaces keeps
   the linear-algebraic content; sheaf cohomology may capture structure pure graph topology
   cannot. Highest-risk, highest-ceiling; most new machinery.
